# Why the pages work the way they do

The template already follows these principles. Keep them in mind when writing content, building a `custom` figure or changing the template.

| # | Principle | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start zoomed out, and open detail on demand. | People need the overview before details mean anything. | Shneiderman, "The Eyes Have It" (1996); the C4 model |
| 2 | One color per role, the same everywhere. | Readers learn the colors once and then read every panel faster. | Mayer's signaling principle; Red Blob Games |
| 3 | Words sit next to what they describe. | Looking back and forth between text and picture uses up attention. | Mayer's contiguity principle |
| 4 | Start paused. The reader controls the pace, and can step back. | Animation helps only when people can control its speed. | Tversky, Morrison & Bétrancourt, "Animation: can it facilitate?" (2002) |
| 5 | Steps, not movies. | People think of events as separate steps. Animate only real motion. | Same paper |
| 6 | Show the values, not just the code. | Readers shouldn't have to imagine what a variable holds. | Bret Victor, "Learnable Programming" |
| 7 | Highlight what changed, for one step. | Changes you can't see cause most confusion. | Victor; Python Tutor |
| 8 | Real code, with honest labels. | Made-up values that look real destroy trust. Tag them as examples. | |
| 9 | Ask the reader to guess before key moments. | Watching alone teaches little. Predicting first makes the answer stick. | Naps et al., visualization engagement study (2002) |
| 10 | Nothing moves around between steps. | If boxes jump around, the reader loses their place. | Red Blob Games |
| 11 | Teach the key words first. | New words learned in advance free up attention for the hard part. | Mayer's pre-training principle |
| 12 | The page makes sense before any click. | Many readers never click. The first view must carry the point. | Archie Tse (NYT); Red Blob Games |
| 13 | Hover shows meaning. | Readers check a term without losing their place. | Victor |
| 14 | One page, one question, one takeaway. | A clear question tells the reader what to look for. | Olah & Carter, "Research Debt" |

## Mistakes to avoid
- Animation that plays on its own with no way to pause or step back.
- A map with every file on it. Show 5 to 12 parts, and more only on click.
- Explanations far from the thing they explain.
- Steps over 40 words.
- Color used for decoration.
- Values that don't match what the real code would hold.
- A page that only makes sense after clicking.
